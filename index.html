From paycheck
to plate.

A small, automatic gift joins a pooled monthly fund. Operating costs stay capped. The rest becomes e-vouchers at African-owned food stores.

  1. 1

    You choose a number you can keep.

    $1 to $10 comes out monthly by payroll deduction, debit card or PayPal. Change or stop it in one click.

  2. 2

    The pool covers the basics first.

    Payment processing and a 7% operating cap come off the top. The published ledger shows both, every month.

  3. 3

    The rest becomes food on the table.

    $10 e-vouchers are sent to verified families and redeemed at partner African groceries. Unspent funds roll into the next batch.

The Sunday pot

Sample story. Houston, Texas.

After church, three households split the cost of fish, tomatoes and rice. A pooled market voucher turns one counter into a Sunday meal for eleven people.

A $10 voucher does not replace a budget. It makes the last week of the month less brittle.

Amina’s Saturday list

Sample story. Minneapolis, Minnesota.

Rice, lentils, tomatoes, tea. The voucher covers the staples her family already cooks, at the Somali market two blocks from home.

The store that says yes

Sample story. Atlanta, Georgia.

A family-run African grocery redeems the vouchers at the till and gets paid weekly. The store stays open, and the block keeps its food counter.
